Dhamma Corner

A week in which the Nasdaq loses a tenth of its value and the S&P 500 falls into correction is a good week to reread the Buddha's teaching on the eight worldly conditions. In the Lokadhamma Sutta, the Buddha says that gain and loss, fame and disrepute, praise and blame, pleasure and pain keep rolling over the world, and the world keeps rolling after them.

The sutta separates two kinds of people. The uninstructed ordinary person meets these conditions without understanding. When gain arrives, the mind leans toward it and holds on. When loss arrives, the mind pushes against it and suffers. The instructed disciple of the noble ones reflects that these conditions are impermanent, unsatisfactory and subject to change. Because of that reflection, the disciple is not captured by them. Notice that the teaching does not ask anyone to stop caring about outcomes or to stop working. It asks us to see clearly that the outcomes are not ours to hold.

For investors and founders, this is practical. A portfolio that was up last month and down this week has not changed in its nature. What changed is the mind's grip. The sutta offers a simple question to ask on a day like Friday: is my mind leaning toward the price, or standing apart from it? Standing apart does not mean being passive. It means making decisions on Monday from clarity rather than from fear, and stopping the habit of measuring self-worth by a number on a screen.

For those who live and work across borders, the same teaching applies to the headlines about summits, tariffs and policy threats. Each of these will come and go. Praise and blame will follow any decision you make, and gain and loss will follow any venture. The Buddha's advice is to cultivate the steady mind that can meet all eight without being thrown by them. This steadiness is built through ordinary practice: keeping precepts, being generous, and sitting quietly each day, even a short while, so that the mind learns to observe its own leaning.

As you open your accounts and your news feeds this weekend, try a brief exercise. Name which of the eight conditions you are facing. Say plainly, "This is loss," or "This is blame." Then ask whether it is permanent. The answer will usually loosen the grip, and from that looser place you can act wisely.

Personal Reflection

This week, a lot of people watched a number fall and felt something fall inside them too. You may have felt it, or you may have felt relief that it was not yours. Either way, notice how quickly a figure on a screen can tell you who you are. Some part of you has been quietly keeping score, and it rarely asks your permission. Underneath the score is the person who wakes up, cares for others, and does honest work. That person was there before Friday's close, and will be there after the next one.

If every number you track went to zero tomorrow, what would still be undeniably, steadily you?
