Dhamma Corner

Today's headlines share a single thread: the hope that a pause, a pledge, or a rally will make things safe. China suspends its export curbs for two months. A rebound adds 1,100 points to the Dow in a day. Governments announce billions that have not yet been spent. Each offers relief, and each is temporary.

The early teachings begin from this observation. In the First Discourse, the Buddha's Dhamma-wheel teaching, known as the Setting in Motion of the Wheel of Dhamma, the Buddha is described as laying out the Four Noble Truths: that there is dukkha, that it has a cause, that it can cease, and that there is a path to its ending. The sutta identifies the cause as craving, including craving for becoming and craving for non-becoming. It does not say that conditions will not change. It says that clinging to them is what hurts.

For a leader or investor, this has a practical edge. Craving for a stable outcome leads us to read a two-month pause as a settlement. It leads us to read a single strong market day as a new era. Craving for non-becoming, the wish that a threat simply go away, leads us to ignore the January deadline until it arrives. Both are ways of leaning away from what is actually in front of us.

The sutta describes a middle way, free of the extremes of indulgence and self-torment. In the language of work, that might mean preparing seriously without panic, and acting without needing the result to be guaranteed. You can diversify suppliers, hold cash reserves, and build skills, without insisting that the world confirm your plan.

The path the sutta lays out begins with right view, seeing things as they are, and right intention, the commitment to act without ill will. For a digital nomad whose life depends on visas, flights, and connections, this is a daily practice. Notice what is changing, accept that it is changing, and choose your next step with care for yourself and others.

One more point deserves mention. The Dhamma is not passivity. The same discourse ends with the path to be developed, not merely understood. Today, development might mean something small: reading a headline once, noticing the urge to react, and waiting a breath before deciding. A steady mind is one of the few assets that no export curb can touch.

Personal Reflection

Today's news is full of pauses: a pause in export curbs, a pause in a market's fall. Most of us live by pauses too. You may be waiting for a project to settle, a relationship to clarify, or a season of uncertainty to end before you let yourself feel at ease.

But the pause rarely comes with a promise. Notice where you have been treating a temporary calm as a permanent answer, and what you might do differently if you assumed it would end.

What are you postponing until things feel safe, and what would it cost you to begin today?
