Dhamma Corner

In an epoch dominated by rapid technological disruption, shifting sovereign bond yields, and the anxiety of structural fragmentation, human attention is relentlessly pulled outward. We calculate risks, monitor algorithmic threat reports, and attempt to fortify our enterprises against unseen volatility. Yet the foundational teaching of the awakened path reminds us that external security is a mirage constructed by a restless mind seeking permanence where none exists.

The Buddha addressed the relentless turbulence of conditioned existence by directing inquiry inward to the nature of craving and clinging. When market forces fluctuate and geopolitical alliances fracture, the untrained mind reacts with fear or compulsive acquisition, mistaking transient phenomena for solid ground. True clarity arises not from mastering every external variable, but from discerning the conditioned nature of all formations. By observing how thoughts of scarcity and ambition arise and pass away, the leader discovers an unshakeable inner stability that depends on no external index or treasury intervention.

Reference: Anatta-lakkhana Sutta (SN 22.59) — On the impermanence, unsatisfactory nature, and non-self characteristic of all conditioned phenomena.
