Dhamma Corner

When global markets convulse, maritime trade routes close, and sovereign debt expands like an unchecked wildfire, the human mind instinctively seeks shelter in frantic calculation. We monitor bond yields, adjust portfolios, and attempt to outguess geopolitical friction points with the illusion of absolute control. Yet the foundational teaching of the Dhamma points precisely to the root cause of this turbulence: the relentless grasping at conditions that are by their very nature impermanent, unsatisfactory, and devoid of a permanent core.

In the Kachchayanagotta Sutta, the Buddha explains that the world largely relies on a duality of attachment—clinging to existence or falling into the nihilism of non-existence. When we view economic security or geopolitical stability as solid, enduring entities, we set ourselves up for inevitable suffering when conditions shift. The wise practitioner does not deny the reality of worldly systems, nor does one ignore the practical duties of leadership and strategy; rather, one perceives conditioned phenomena with radical equanimity. When you see that supply chains, state power, and market valuations rise and fall according to dependent origination, the mind ceases to be tossed about by every macroeconomic shock.

For the modern decision-maker, innovator, or digital nomad, this realization is not an invitation to passivity, but the ultimate source of unshakeable clarity. True strategic agility requires a mind that is unburdened by reactive panic. By understanding that all fabricated things dissolve, you act with precision, compassion, and uncompromised integrity—fully engaged in the work of the world, yet internally untangled from its storms.

Reference: SN 12.15 Kachchayanagotta Sutta (Connected Discourses of the Buddha)
