Dhamma Corner

In periods of intense macroeconomic volatility, when geopolitical chokepoints threaten global trade and energy markets fluctuate wildly, the human mind instinctively searches for solid ground. We build elaborate forecasts, stockpile capital, and erect psychological defenses against uncertainty. Yet the foundational teaching of the Dhamma points to an uncomfortable truth: the external world is inherently unstable, characterized by relentless flux, friction, and change.

When leaders, innovators, and market movers react to sudden shocks with anxiety or aggressive resistance, they suffer twice. First from the objective disruption—be it a stalled shipping lane, a soaring energy bill, or an agricultural drought—and second from the mental agitation that insists reality ought to conform to expectations. The Buddha continually reminded his followers that clinging to what is unstable, conditioned, and subject to change inevitably breeds suffering.

True strategic clarity does not arise from controlling every variable in a complex global ecosystem, but from developing a quiet, unshakeable equanimity amidst turbulence. When you observe market shifts or supply chain fractures without immediate reactivity, a space opens between stimulus and response. In that pristine space lies wise action. By abandoning the illusion of absolute permanence and embracing the conditioned nature of worldly affairs, leaders can navigate crises with a cool head, compassionate precision, and enduring effectiveness.
