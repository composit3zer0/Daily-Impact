Dhamma Corner

In periods of intense global volatility—where supply chains fracture overnight, technological dependencies threaten national sovereignty, and natural disasters remind us of our fragile physical footing—the untrained mind instinctively reacts with agitation and grasping. We seek absolute security in external arrangements, building elaborate fortresses of wealth, compliance protocols, and strategic hedges, only to discover that the ground beneath our feet is as shifting as the Flores coastline.

The early Buddhist teachings do not offer a political blueprint to resolve macroeconomic overcapacity or geopolitical friction. Instead, the Dhamma directs our gaze inward to the primary engine of all suffering: the unexamined reactivity of the heart. The Buddha repeatedly emphasized that conditioned phenomena are characterized by impermanence (anicca), unsatisfactoriness (dukkha), and non-self (anattā). To cling to stability in a world defined by change is an exercise in futility.

For the modern leader, innovator, or mobile strategist, true power does not lie in controlling every external variable, but in cultivating an unshakable inner poise. When you observe market turbulence, regulatory shifts, or technological disruption without immediate aversion or craving, you develop discernment (paññā). You see things as they actually are rather than how fear portrays them. By anchoring your awareness in the present moment, you meet complexity with clarity, responding with skillful action rather than impulsive reaction.

Reference: Anatta-lakkhana Sutta (SN 22.59) — On seeing the impermanent and non-self nature of all conditioned elements, leading to disenchantment, detachment, and ultimate liberation from turbulence.
