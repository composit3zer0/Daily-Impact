Dhamma Corner

In moments when global markets heave under sudden regulatory shocks, technological disruptions, and climate-driven resource strains, the human mind instinctively searches for solid ground. We build elaborate forecasts, erect regulatory ramparts, and seek security in material expansion. Yet the early Buddhist teachings repeatedly remind us that external conditions are inherently unstable—composed of changing elements that arise and pass away according to conditions beyond our absolute control.

In the Sela Sutta, the Buddha speaks of the inner refuge that remains unshaken amidst the turbulence of the world. Leaders, innovators, and strategists often invest immense energy into mastering external systems while neglecting the inner apparatus through which all experience is filtered. When greed, aversion, and delusion govern the mind, even vast wealth or technological supremacy becomes a source of anxiety. True strategic resilience—what ancient texts describe as unshakeable liberation of mind—begins when we stop clinging to phenomena that are impermanent by nature.

Practicing mindfulness in an age of acceleration does not mean withdrawing from innovation or market engagement. Rather, it means acting with complete clarity and compassion while remaining unattached to outcomes. When you observe market volatility or geopolitical friction, recognize them as natural phenomena rolling across the screen of consciousness. By cultivating a mind that does not shake when touched by loss or gain, praise or blame, you secure a calm harbor that no external crisis can breach.

Reference: Snp 3.7 Sela Sutta — SuttaCentral
