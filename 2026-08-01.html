Dhamma Corner

In moments when global markets tremble, supply chains fracture, and the drumbeats of conflict echo across regions, the human mind instinctively seeks refuge in control. We draft strategic plans, fortify digital infrastructure, and construct regulatory shields, hoping to master an unpredictable world. Yet the early Buddhist teachings direct our attention inward, recognizing that the turbulence of our external environment is intimately mirrored by the restlessness within.

The Buddha frequently reminded his disciples that the world is led by the mind and swept along by mental formations. When greed, hatred, and delusion animate the collective consciousness of nations and corporations, our most sophisticated technologies—whether AI algorithms, automated agricultural vision systems, or energy grids—invariably become instruments of entanglement rather than liberation. To navigate rapid global change without losing inner clarity, one must cultivate radical discernment, distinguishing between what can be managed through skillful effort and what must be met with unshakeable equanimity.

This practice is not passive resignation; rather, it is the supreme tactical advantage for a leader or innovator. By observing the arising and passing of mental states—desire for security, aversion to loss, anxiety over the unknown—you loosen their grip. When the mind is no longer hijacked by reactivity, action becomes precise, compassionate, and effective. You act not out of panic or defensive posturing, but from a quiet center that remains undisturbed, exactly like deep water unaffected by surface ripples.
