Dhamma Corner

In an economic and geopolitical landscape defined by sudden volatility, shifting monetary regimes, and the relentless pressure to forecast the unpredictable future, the human mind instinctively searches for solid ground. We build elaborate hedges, analyze earnings reports under microscopes, and attempt to outmaneuver systemic risk, driven by an underlying anxiety that our security might vanish overnight. Yet, the foundational teaching of the Buddha directs our attention away from external contingencies and toward the internal architecture of craving and clinging.

In the Samyutta Nikaya, specifically within the analysis of the roots of suffering, the Buddha expounded upon how beings are perpetually bound by their identification with the impermanent. When market indices surge or contract, when supply chains fracture under the weight of geopolitical friction, or when corporate hypes dissolve into reality checks, the common denominator of distress is not the external event itself, but the mind's rigid demand that conditioned phenomena remain stable and secure.

For the modern leader, investor, or nomad navigating high-stakes environments, the practice of Dhamma does not advocate withdrawal from engagement; rather, it offers the supreme liberation of non-attachment amidst activity. To act skillfully in the world requires clear vision—seeing market shifts, technological limits, and political realignments precisely as they are, without the distortion of greed, aversion, or delusion. When you understand that all fabricated things are subject to decay, you engage with strategy and enterprise with total clarity, unburdened by the terror of loss.
