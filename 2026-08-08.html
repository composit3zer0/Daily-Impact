Dhamma Corner

In periods when global systems fluctuate wildly and executives shorten planning horizons in search of safety, the human mind instinctively clings to control. We seek out certainty in policy shifts, market forecasts, and technological buffers, hoping to insulate ourselves against unpredictable external currents. Yet early Buddhist teaching consistently points to a fundamental truth: the conditioned world is inherently unstable, characterized by constant change (anicca) and stress (dukkha).

When the Buddha addressed monks and lay leaders alike, he did not advise them to manipulate external conditions into permanent perfection. Instead, he directed their attention inward to the unshakeable refuge of a trained mind. True stability is not found in static environments or flawless geopolitical forecasts, but in the cessation of grasping. When one understands that pleasant and unpleasant conditions arise and pass away dependent upon causes, the compulsion to panic over shifting circumstances dissolves.

For innovators, decision-makers, and modern wanderers navigating rapid global transformation, this Dhamma perspective offers profound practical liberation. Operational resilience requires clear-sighted action, but inner peace requires letting go of the demand that life conform to our preferred blueprints. By anchoring awareness in the present moment—observing phenomena simply as they arise and dissolve—you cultivate a quiet equipoise that remains unaffected by the noise of the marketplace.

Reference: SN 35.88: Paṭhamaevadassana Sutta — On abandoning identification with impermanent phenomena and cultivating dispassion toward all conditioned things.
