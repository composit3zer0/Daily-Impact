Dhamma Corner

In an era defined by hyper-accelerated capital flows, structural tech layoffs, and volatile geopolitical maneuvers, human attention is constantly pulled toward external turbulence. Markets surge and contract, artificial intelligence reshapes the professional landscape overnight, and leaders find themselves reacting to an endless stream of urgent demands. Yet the foundational teaching of the Buddha reminds us that the root of all agitation lies not in the external world's complexity, but in the unexamined reactivity of the mind.

When the corporate or political environment shifts violently—reminiscent of nations facing sudden trade squeezes or supply chain disruptions—the untrained mind experiences immediate aversion or grasping. We cling desperately to stability, forgetting the fundamental law of impermanence (anicca). What rises must fall; systems evolve, transform, and dissolve. The Buddha observed in the Salla Sutta that the world is afflicted by death and decay, yet the wise do not grieve, having understood the nature of the world. When an innovator or leader views professional displacement or market volatility through the lens of Dhamma, panic gives way to strategic clarity.

Equanimity (upekkhā) is not passive indifference; it is the supreme stabilization of awareness that allows for skillful action. Just as Southeast Asian economies adapt flexibly to shifting global trade currents without losing their sovereign footing, the contemplative leader navigates disruption by remaining anchored in mindfulness. By observing the arising and passing of mental states—greed for high valuations, fear of obsolescence, anger at systemic injustice—without identifying with them, one preserves the inner freedom necessary to make wise, compassionate decisions.
