Dhamma Corner

In periods of sweeping global turbulence—where trade routes close, technological shifts displace human labor, and environmental extremes alter our landscapes—the human mind instinctively seeks stability in external arrangements. We look to secure energy contracts, hedge against tariff escalations, or optimize corporate structures to insulate ourselves from sudden shocks. Yet the Dhamma reminds us that all conditioned phenomena are inherently impermanent, subject to change, and devoid of an enduring core.

When the Buddha addressed the nature of worldly volatility, he frequently directed attention inward, observing that suffering arises not from external conditions themselves, but from our grasping at things that cannot be held. The rapids of the Vistula River may recede, geopolitical choke points may shift, and corporate markets may fluctuate wildly, but the observer within remains untouched unless it identifies with these passing storms. True security is never found in the rearrangement of impermanent conditions; it is discovered through the unshakeable liberation of a mind that no longer clings.
