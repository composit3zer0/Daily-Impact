Dhamma Corner

Today's headlines share a common feature: claims arrive faster than confirmation. A rare earth suspension may expire on one date or another. A negotiation is under way, but its result is unknown. Supply figures move weekly, and every number is already stale. Leaders, investors and travellers must act without certainty, and the question is how.

The Kalama Sutta of the Anguttara Nikaya addresses a similar problem. The Kalamas, villagers visited by many teachers who contradicted one another, ask whom to believe. In the sutta, the Buddha advises them not to accept a view merely because of report, tradition, hearsay, scripture, logic, or the standing of the speaker. Instead they should observe for themselves which states of mind lead to harm and suffering and which lead to welfare and happiness, and then abandon the former and take up the latter. The teaching is not a licence for cynicism. It is an invitation to test claims against lived results.

For a decision-maker, this has a practical reading. Before acting on a headline, notice what is driving the urge to act. Greed shows up as the fear of missing a rally. Aversion shows up as the impulse to panic-sell or to blame. Delusion shows up as certainty built on a single source. The sutta names these three roots, and a trader or founder who can recognise them in the moment has a real edge.

The sutta also points to the wise person's standard: choices that bring benefit to oneself and others, and that the wise would praise. A supply-chain decision that quietly shifts risk onto weaker partners, or a payment scheme that hides its costs from users, fails this test even if it is profitable. Early Buddhist ethics treats livelihood as part of the path, and right livelihood means work that does not depend on harm or deceit.

For the digital nomad, the same teaching offers steadiness. Visa rules, flight prices and regional health advisories change often. Gather information from several sources, hold plans loosely, and keep your inner state from being dictated by the latest alert. Do not confuse restlessness with freedom. Contentment with what is sufficient is praised throughout the early discourses as a true form of wealth.

Today, then, practise a small discipline: before any significant decision, pause for three breaths and ask what you actually know, what you are assuming, and what state of mind is doing the asking.

Eating less protein could slow aging, review of 350 studies finds
Source: ScienceDaily · Published: 10 Nov 2026 · Reading time: 6 minutes
Summary: A review of more than 350 papers argues that many people may be eating more protein than they need, and that cutting back could slow biological aging. Dudley Lamming and a colleague identified recurring mechanisms: improved metabolism, altered cellular nutrient sensing, less cellular damage and better cell maintenance. One standout is the hormone FGF21, which rises when protein is limited and, in mice, is linked to longer life. Several human trials show lower protein can reduce body fat and fasting blood sugar. Crucially, the authors do not claim lifespan extension in humans, and they concede protein helps muscle in active people and older adults. 6. Sex and Relationships
Don't Let Conflict Erode Your Relationship
Source: Psychology Today · Published: 10 Nov 2026 · Reading time: 5 minutes
Summary: Couples can fight over trivial things, but new research suggests the memory of a conflict can matter as much as the quarrel itself. Aurélie Thuot-Jolicoeur and colleagues, drawing on Self-Determination Theory, asked participants to recall a significant dispute with a partner and rated their emotion regulation. Those who showed integrative regulation, noticing and accepting unpleasant feelings to learn from them, reported higher relationship satisfaction than those who were dysregulated through hostility, withdrawal or rumination. The practical lesson is to treat a resurfacing memory like the original argument: acknowledge the feeling, get curious about your partner's perspective. Because the evidence is correlational, it shows association, not cause.

But there is a part of you that wants certainty before it will allow itself to rest, and that part may never be satisfied. Underneath the forecasting, who are you when nothing is settled?
