Dhamma Corner

In moments when global supply chains rupture, energy markets convulse, and technological systems demand our relentless attention, the human mind easily falls prey to agitation. We mistake the shifting projections of the economic and political spheres for solid reality, binding our peace of mind to external conditions that are by their very nature impermanent, unsatisfactory, and non-self. The early Buddhist teachings remind us that seeking security in conditioned phenomena is like trying to build a fortress upon shifting ocean sand.

The Buddha frequently pointed out that the root of human suffering lies not in the external storms—whether solar eclipses, closed straits, or market crashes—but in the mental reactivity we attach to them. When greed, aversion, and delusion take hold, we experience a dual imprisonment: physically affected by circumstance and mentally imprisoned by our own resistance to it. True refuge requires cultivating an inner stillness that remains unshakeable amidst worldly winds. By training the attention to observe sensations and thoughts with equanimity rather than clinging or pushing away, one develops a resilient clarity that no geopolitical disruption can diminish.
