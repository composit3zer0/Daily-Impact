Dhamma Corner

In times when geopolitical friction rattles global markets, missile systems pierce regional skies, and structural economic divergences widen, the human mind instinctively searches for a solid rock to cling to. We look to institutional stability, robust asset portfolios, or technological dominance for security. Yet early Buddhist teachings direct our attention toward a more radical realization: true refuge cannot be found in external conditions that are inherently unstable, conditioned, and subject to decay.

The Buddha frequently reminded his disciples that the world of conditioned phenomena is marked by constant change, unsatisfactoriness, and non-self. When executives and innovators chase certainty in fluctuating markets, they experience stress precisely because they demand permanence from the impermanent. Developing inner resilience does not mean ignoring external realities or strategic risks; rather, it means cultivating a mind that remains unshakeable amidst gain and loss, praise and blame, success and failure.

By anchoring awareness in the present moment through mindfulness and ethical clarity, leaders can make sound, compassionate decisions without being swept away by emotional reactivity or panic. When you understand that every market cycle, geopolitical standoff, and technological wave arises and passes away, you cultivate the equanimity necessary to act with wisdom rather than fear.

Reference: SN 35.28: Ādittapariyāya Sutta (The Fire Sermon) — Detailed discourse on how all conditioned senses and phenomena are burning with the fires of attachment, aversion, and delusion, and the necessity of detachment for liberation.
