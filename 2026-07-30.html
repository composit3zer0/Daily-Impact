Dhamma Corner

In periods when geopolitical friction shakes energy markets, supply chains, and individual security, the human mind naturally reacts with agitation and anxiety. We grasp at stability, treating external fluctuations as direct threats to our well-being. Yet early Buddhist teaching consistently points out that the conditioned world is inherently unstable—subject to incessant arising and passing away.

The Buddha emphasized that true refuge is not found in controlling external conditions, which remain perpetually beyond our dominion, but in training the heart to remain undisturbed amidst changing tides. When an executive or innovator experiences market volatility or institutional uncertainty, the immediate impulse is reactive rumination. Mindfulness acts as the anchor, cutting through mental proliferation by observing sensations and thoughts with equanimity.

By cultivating a discerning awareness that recognizes all phenomena as impermanent, you develop a resilient clarity. You act decisively in the world without being consumed by its turmoil, realizing that inner peace is an independent dimension of awareness unaffected by external storms.

Reference: Samyutta Nikaya 35.28 (Loka Sutta) — SuttaCentral SN 35.28
