Dhamma Corner

This week showed how quickly the ground can move. Oil fell sixteen percent in five days, bitcoin lost five percent in an afternoon, and a vice president rewrote the story of an election. For anyone who allocates capital, leads a team or works from a laptop in a borrowed city, the sensation is familiar: certainty gives way, and the mind scrambles for a new footing.

The early teachings speak to this directly. In the Sallatha Sutta, the Dart discourse of the Saṁyutta Nikāya, the Buddha is reported to describe how an untrained person, struck by pain, is like someone shot by one arrow and then by a second. The first arrow is the event itself, the loss or the shock. The second is the mind's reaction: resentment, panic, and the story that this should not be happening. The trained disciple, the sutta says, feels the first arrow but is spared the second.

Consider a liquidated trader. The market moved, and that is the first arrow. The second is the rehearsal that follows: replaying the entry, blaming a regulator, resolving to win it back with more leverage. Likewise, a founder who hears of a data center moratorium feels the first arrow of a real constraint, and may then add a second by catastrophizing the whole business. The teaching does not ask for indifference. It asks for accurate seeing, so that action springs from clarity and not from the sting.

The same discourse links this to impermanence. Feelings of pleasure, pain and neither arise and pass away. A rally and a crash are conditions, and neither is a self. A leader who identifies with the price, holding that "my worth rose today," has tied the heart to something that cannot hold still. Peace talks, tariffs and election results are likewise conditioned things, arising from causes and fading with them.

Practically, the sutta suggests a simple discipline for a volatile Monday. Notice the first arrow without delay: name the sensation in the body, the tightness or the heat. Then notice the second: the story and the urge. Pause before trading, sending the angry message, or booking the panicked flight. Decide from the steadier place that remains once the second arrow is left unthrown. Keep ethical conduct as your constant, honesty in what you report and restraint in what you promise, because it is the one asset the market cannot reprice.

For the mobile professional, far from family and routine, this practice is portable. It needs no equipment, only willingness to feel what is present and see it clearly. Meet the week's turbulence with that, and the pain that arrives will be only as large as the event itself.
