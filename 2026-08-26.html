Dhamma Corner

In a world pulled apart by trade disputes, intelligence maneuvering, and technological disruption, the human mind frantically seeks stable ground. We look to surging asset prices, institutional prediction desks, and regional compacts to provide security against an unpredictable horizon. Yet the Buddha consistently directed seekers away from external conditions as a source of refuge, teaching instead that the conditioned world is inherently unstable, marked by constant change.

When leaders and innovators build systems—whether securing artificial intelligence or negotiating maritime shipping routes—they operate within the realm of formations. Without inner clarity, the drive for control generates anxiety and friction. The practice of mindfulness is the antidote to this reactive rushing. By observing the arising and passing of thoughts without clinging, one develops an inner fortress that no geopolitical shock or market correction can dismantle.

True stability is not found in a static world, but in a mind that has transcended reactivity. When you recognize that external phenomena are transient, peace arises naturally from non-attachment.

Reference: SN 35.28: Ādittapariyāya Sutta (The Fire Sermon) — Expounding how the all-burning nature of sensory and worldly experience leads the wise to disenchantment and liberation from reactivity.
