Dhamma Corner

In periods of intense global volatility—when electoral outcomes hang in the balance, supply chains face disruption, and technological shifts threaten traditional livelihoods—the human mind instinctively seeks refuge in control. We scrutinize market tickers, analyze geopolitical maneuvers, and attempt to engineer certainty out of an inherently unstable environment. Yet the foundational teachings of the early Buddhist tradition remind us that clinging to external conditions for security is an exercise in building houses upon shifting sand.

The Buddha frequently directed his disciples to examine the transient nature of all conditioned phenomena. In the Samyutta Nikaya, he observed that whatever is impermanent is subject to change, and what is subject to change is ultimately unsatisfactory. When decision-makers and innovators tie their inner peace to the success of enterprise expansions, political elections, or flawless security architectures, they invite perpetual anxiety. True clarity does not emerge from mastering the external world, but from understanding the mechanics of craving and aversion within one's own awareness.

Cultivating unshakeable presence amidst rapid global change requires the practice of radical equanimity—not as passive resignation, but as an active, lucid engagement with reality as it is rather than as we wish it to be. When an executive or digital nomad learns to observe market turbulence and technological disruption without immediate emotional reactivity, a profound inner spaciousness arises. In that stillness, wise action becomes possible, unclouded by panic or greed.

Reference: Anicca Vagga, Samyutta Nikaya (SN 22.45).
