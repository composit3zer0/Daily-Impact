Dhamma Corner

This week, markets lurched on a single statement from a podium. Oil fell nearly a tenth, then climbed back. Chip stocks lost trillions in a day, and Bitcoin funds recorded their worst week since June. For anyone who allocates capital or builds a business, the swings are a daily teacher.

The early Buddhist texts describe this terrain precisely. In the Lokadhamma Sutta, the Buddha, as recorded in the Anguttara Nikaya, lists eight worldly conditions that keep the world turning: gain and loss, fame and disrepute, praise and blame, pleasure and pain. The sutta says that the uninstructed person is carried away by these, elated by gain and crushed by loss. The instructed disciple understands that they are impermanent, unsatisfying and subject to change, and so is not swept along.

Notice what the teaching does not say. It does not tell us to avoid markets, ambition or work. It asks us to see the conditions as conditions. A rally is gain, a selloff is loss, and a headline is praise or blame arriving from a distance. None of them is a verdict on who you are.

For a leader, this is practical. Decisions made in elation tend to overreach and decisions made in dread tend to freeze. The sutta invites a pause between the event and the reaction, a moment in which you can ask what is actually known and what is only feared or hoped. That pause is what the tradition calls mindfulness, and it has an ethical edge as well: those who depend on your judgement are affected by the state of mind from which you decide.

For the digital nomad, whose income, residence and routine can all shift quickly, the same lesson applies. Comfort that depends on the exchange rate, the visa or the platform is borrowed comfort. The texts point to a steadier base in generosity, virtue and a clear mind, things no selloff can reprice.

Try a small practice today. The next time you check a price or a headline and feel a pull of excitement or alarm, name it silently as one of the eight conditions. Then ask whether you need to act now, or only to remember that this too will change.
